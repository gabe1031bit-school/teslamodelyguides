01

When to Charge

Electricity has rush hour pricing too — plugging in at the right time is the single biggest lever you control.

Check for time-of-use (TOU) rates
BIGGEST LEVER

Ask your utility about TOU plans

Many utilities charge 2–4x more for power during evening peak hours (often 4–9pm) than overnight. Switching to a TOU plan and charging late at night can meaningfully cut your per-mile cost.

Schedule charging, don't "just plug in"

Set a charging schedule in the car (Controls > Charging) or the app so it waits for off-peak hours automatically, even if you plug in as soon as you get home.

Use "Scheduled Departure" for a warm start

Instead of charging the moment you plug in, tell the car when you're leaving — it'll finish charging and precondition the cabin/battery right on time, off-peak.

Avoid the 4–9pm window

This is peak demand for most grids in the U.S. — the worst possible time to pull extra amps if you have any flexibility at all.

How full, how often
1Daily drivingCharge to 50–80% overnight at home — most owners rarely need a full batteryCheapest & battery-friendly
2Day before a road tripBump the limit to 90–100% the night before you actually need the rangeOccasional use only
3Topping off at a SuperchargerCharge to what gets you to the next stop, not to 100% — speed drops sharply above ~80%Faster & cheaper

💡 Rule of thumb

Home charging is almost always your cheapest option per mile, off-peak home charging is cheaper still, and Supercharging is for trips — not routine top-ups.

02

Home Charging Setup

Get the hardware decision right once and every charge after that is basically free effort.

Wall Connector (Level 2)

Fastest home option — adds a full charge overnight. Worth the install cost if you drive daily or plan to keep the car long-term.

Mobile Connector + NEMA 14-50

A cheaper middle ground: a standard high-power outlet (like a dryer outlet) plus the portable connector that ships with the car.

SLOWEST

Standard 120V outlet

Works in a pinch (a few miles of range per hour) but is rarely enough for daily driving unless your commute is very short.

Get 2–3 electrician quotes

Wall Connector installs vary widely by panel capacity and run length — always compare quotes rather than taking the first one.

Ask about a dedicated EV meter

Some utilities offer a discounted EV-only rate if you install a second meter just for the charger — worth asking about before you install anything.

Check your panel capacity first

An electrician can tell you in minutes whether your existing panel supports a Wall Connector or needs an upgrade — cheaper to know upfront.

03

Supercharging Smart

Fast charging is convenient by design — a few habits keep it from being expensive by accident.

Charge to what you need, not 100%

Charging speed tapers dramatically past ~80% — stopping there instead of waiting for 100% saves both time and money on a per-minute-priced session.

Let navigation pick the stop

Routing an in-car trip automatically chooses efficient Supercharger stops and preconditions the battery for faster charging on arrival.

WATCH FOR

Idle fees

Some Supercharger stations charge a per-minute idle fee if you leave the car plugged in after it's finished charging — move it once you're done, especially when busy.

Off-peak Supercharging pricing

Pricing at some stations varies by time of day and local demand — the in-car map shows the live rate at each station before you commit.

Charge during your stop, not for your stop

Plan a bathroom/food break around the charge time rather than sitting and waiting — it doesn't cost more, but it feels like it costs less.

Non-Tesla stations, checked in-app

The Tesla app and in-car map both show nearby non-Tesla fast chargers your car supports — worth comparing pricing when you're off the beaten path.

04

Rebates & Incentives

The federal purchase credit landscape changed in 2025 — here's what's actually left to check.

CHANGED IN 2025

Federal EV purchase credit

The federal new/used EV purchase tax credits ended for vehicles acquired after September 30, 2025. If you bought before that date under a binding contract, you may still qualify — otherwise, it no longer applies to new purchases.

Home charger credit (Section 30C)

A federal credit for home EV charging equipment has had its own, separate deadline — confirm current status and eligibility with a tax professional before assuming it still applies.

STILL WORTH CHECKING

State & utility incentives

Many states and local utilities still offer their own EV rebates, reduced registration fees, HOV lane access, or discounted charging rates — these are separate from federal programs and change often.

Check before, not after

Incentive programs shift quickly. Always verify current eligibility on your state energy office's site or with a tax professional rather than relying on last year's numbers.

Referral rewards

Tesla periodically runs owner-referral programs with perks for both the referrer and the new buyer — check the "Referrals" section of the app since offers rotate.

05

Everyday Money-Savers

Ownership costs beyond the electricity bill — and where there's real room to trim them.

Shop insurance annually

EV insurance rates vary a lot by carrier. Compare quotes yearly, and ask specifically whether the insurer has EV/Tesla experience — repair cost estimates differ widely.

Usage-based insurance options

Some insurers (including Tesla's own insurance product, where available) price partly on your real driving behavior — smoother, more cautious driving can lower your premium over time.

Rotate your own tires

Tire rotation is one of the few truly DIY-friendly maintenance items — many independent shops handle it cheaply if you'd rather not, without needing a Tesla-specific technician.

Skip unnecessary "EV services"

No oil changes, no transmission fluid, no spark plugs — be skeptical of any shop quoting you for maintenance items a Tesla simply doesn't have.

Try before you buy FSD

Full Self-Driving is available as a trial, monthly subscription, or one-time purchase — the subscription lets you test it for a month or two before committing to the larger upfront cost.

Time software-linked purchases

Pricing and trial availability for features like FSD change periodically — if you're on the fence, it costs nothing to check current pricing in the app before deciding.

Genuine accessories, bought once

Cheap third-party mats or covers that don't fit right can trap moisture or interfere with sensors — often costing more in the long run than buying the right part once.

06

Battery & Range Longevity

The battery is the most expensive part of the car — these habits keep it healthy for the long run.

DAILY HABIT

Keep it between ~20–80%

For everyday driving, a moderate daily charge limit is easier on long-term battery health than habitually charging to 100% or running it down near empty.

Stay plugged in when parked long-term

Leaving the car connected (even without actively charging) during extended time away helps the battery management system maintain a healthy state — better than letting it sit at a very low charge.

Precondition before fast charging

Letting navigation route you to a Supercharger warms the battery in advance, which charges faster and more efficiently than showing up cold.

Moderate driving stretches range

Smooth acceleration and steady speeds do more for real-world range than any single setting — aggressive driving is the biggest efficiency killer.

Keep tires properly inflated

Underinflated tires increase rolling resistance and quietly eat into both range and tire life — a monthly pressure check pays for itself.

Extreme temperatures cost range temporarily

Very hot or cold weather reduces efficiency and available regen — this is normal battery chemistry, not a fault, and usually recovers once temperatures moderate.

07

App & Software Tricks

Free features that are already sitting in your car or your phone.

Energy app

Shows real trip-by-trip and long-term efficiency, helping you spot exactly which habits (routes, speeds, climate use) are costing you the most range.

Charging cost estimates

The in-car map and app both show an estimated cost before you start a Supercharging session, so there's rarely a surprise on the bill.

Software update release notes

Always worth a quick read — new updates regularly add small efficiency, charging, or convenience improvements at no extra cost.

Schedule updates for overnight

Set updates to install while you sleep instead of interrupting a weekend afternoon — the car will remind you when one's ready either way.

Share keys, not passwords

Add trusted drivers as Phone Keys with adjustable permissions instead of handing over your login — safer, and easy to revoke later.

Set a charge-limit reminder

If you only occasionally need a full charge, the app can prompt you before a long trip so you're not stuck adjusting the limit at 6am.

08

General Owner Tips

The small habits that prevent 90% of "wait, is this normal?" moments.

Clean camera lenses regularly

A dirty fender or bumper camera can quietly disable Autopilot and safety features — wipe them down with the rest of your regular car care.

COMMON MYTH

"Sentry Mode drains the battery fast"

It does use some energy while parked, but for most owners it's a small daily draw, not a dealbreaker — turn it off in very low-traffic private parking if you're range-conscious.

Don't ignore small alerts

Tap "Learn More" on any touchscreen alert rather than dismissing it — most are informational, but a few (like tire pressure) are worth acting on quickly.

Winter-proof the charge port

In icy climates, a quick check that the charge port door isn't frozen shut saves a frustrating morning — a soft cloth and patience beats force.

Use garage mode features if you have a garage

Smart Garage (HomeLink) setup takes five minutes once and saves you fumbling for a separate remote every single day after.

Set up Valet Mode before handing over keys

Anytime someone else is driving without you — a valet, a shop, a teen driver's first solo trip — it takes seconds and protects your data and settings.

Know your manual door releases

Find the mechanical backups for your doors and frunk once, calmly, rather than discovering them for the first time during a power issue.

09

Road Trip Prep

A little planning turns "range anxiety" into just... a normal trip.

Let the car plan the route

Enter your final destination before you leave — the in-car navigation automatically inserts Supercharger stops and estimates arrival battery %.

Charge to 90–100% the night before

Only for the day you're actually road-tripping — no need to keep the battery at a high charge level day-to-day otherwise.

Check tire pressure before a long haul

Cold mornings and long highway stretches both affect pressure — a quick check the day before avoids a mid-trip warning.

Download offline maps for dead zones

Handy for remote stretches with weak cell coverage, so navigation doesn't stall out right when you need it most.

Pack the mobile connector anyway

Even on a Supercharger-heavy route, a backup way to trickle-charge from a regular outlet is good insurance for the unexpected detour.

Use Theater during your charging stop

A 20–30 minute Supercharger stop is a good excuse for a coffee, a walk, or a few minutes of streaming video right from the car.

Resale & Long-Term Value

Small habits now that pay off if and when you ever sell or trade in.

Keep charging habits moderate

A battery that's been kept in a healthy daily range tends to show better health metrics down the line than one that's lived at extremes.

Protect the interior early

Seat covers or a simple cargo liner in the first few months cost little and go a long way toward keeping factory upholstery in trade-in-ready shape.

Keep a maintenance record

Even with minimal scheduled maintenance, saving service and tire records in the app gives a future buyer confidence in how the car was cared for.

Understand transferability before you buy add-ons

Some software features transfer with the car when sold and some don't — worth checking current policy before assuming an add-on adds resale value.

Time the market if you can

Used EV values can move with new-model pricing changes and incentive news — if you have flexibility on timing a sale, it's worth a quick look at recent comparable listings first.
