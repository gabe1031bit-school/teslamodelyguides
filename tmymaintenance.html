01

Daily Care Basics

Most "maintenance" is really just a handful of habits repeated consistently.

ZERO COST

No oil, no fluids, no belts

There's no engine oil, transmission fluid, spark plugs, or timing belt to worry about — the traditional maintenance schedule simply doesn't apply.

Wipe cameras and sensors monthly

A quick pass with a soft cloth on the cameras and sensors keeps Autopilot and safety features working as intended — dirt and grime are the most common cause of a disabled feature.

Check tire pressure monthly

Temperature swings change pressure more than people expect — a monthly check (more often in winter) prevents both handling issues and uneven wear.

Replace cabin air filter periodically

This is one of the few scheduled parts on the car — check your specific model's recommended interval and swap it yourself or at service.

02

Cleaning In & Out

What keeps the car looking new — and what quietly damages it.

Hand wash over automatic, when possible

Touchless or hand washes are gentler on the paint and trim than brush-style automatic washes, which can leave fine scratches over time.

CHECK FIRST

Confirm car wash mode before an automatic wash

Putting the car in car wash mode closes windows, folds mirrors, and disables sensors that might otherwise react badly to a wash bay — always enable it first.

Avoid harsh interior cleaners

Strong solvents and ammonia-based cleaners can damage the interior's finish and touchscreen coating — use manufacturer-recommended or mild, non-abrasive products instead.

Vacuum tracks and seat rails

Grit that collects in seat tracks and door tracks can cause noise or premature wear — an occasional vacuum in those spots goes a long way.

Protect the glass roof

Standard automotive glass cleaner is fine, but avoid abrasive pads — the roof is glass, not painted metal, and scratches easily with the wrong tools.

Don't neglect the trunk and frunk seals

Wiping down door and trunk seals occasionally prevents dirt buildup that can eventually affect the seal and let in noise or moisture.

03

Tires & Wheels

EVs are harder on tires than gas cars — this is the maintenance item most owners underestimate.

Why it matters more on an EV
WATCH FOR

Instant torque wears tires faster

The immediate, full-power delivery of an electric motor puts more stress on tires than a comparable gas car — expect somewhat shorter tire life, especially with spirited driving.

Extra weight from the battery

The battery pack adds meaningful weight, which increases rolling load on tires — proper inflation matters even more than usual.

Keeping ahead of it
1Rotate on a scheduleFront and rear tires wear differently — rotating on a consistent interval evens it outDIY-friendly
2Check pressure monthlyUnderinflation is the single biggest avoidable cause of premature wearFree, 2 minutes
3Get an alignment check yearlyEspecially after hitting a pothole hard or noticing uneven wearWorth the visit

💡 Rule of thumb

Smoother acceleration doesn't just save range — it's the single biggest thing you control that extends tire life.

04

Software & Systems Checks

A few minutes in the app or on the touchscreen catches most issues early.

Review the Service section in-app

The app surfaces any open recalls, service bulletins, or recommended items tied to your specific car — worth a glance every few months.

Keep software reasonably current

Updates periodically include bug fixes for exactly the kind of quirky glitch owners tend to worry about — don't defer them indefinitely.

Run a 12V battery check if prompted

Like any car, there's a small 12V battery for low-voltage systems — if the car ever flags it, don't ignore the alert, as it can affect the ability to start up.

Test windows and mirrors periodically

Cycling windows, mirrors, and the frunk/trunk occasionally helps you notice early if something feels off, before it becomes a bigger repair.

05

Seasonal Prep

A little prep before each season change prevents most weather-related annoyances.

Swap to winter tires below ~45°F regularly

All-season tires lose grip in sustained cold — if you see real winter weather, dedicated winter tires make a noticeable difference in braking and handling.

Check wiper blades before rainy season

Wiper blades degrade with UV exposure and age — replace them before the season you'll actually need them, not after they've started streaking.

Clear vents and drains before fall

Leaves and debris can collect around cowl vents — a quick clear-out before autumn prevents water pooling issues.

Pre-cool or pre-heat before extreme days

Preconditioning while still plugged in uses grid power instead of battery charge, which is both more efficient and easier on the battery in extreme heat or cold.

06

Warning Lights, Decoded

Most touchscreen alerts are informational — a few are worth acting on quickly.

ACT SOON

Tire pressure warning

Don't dismiss and forget — check pressure at your earliest convenience, since a slow leak can turn into a flat.

ACT SOON

Reduced regenerative braking

Common in cold weather while the battery warms up — usually resolves on its own after some driving, but persistent warnings outside cold weather are worth a service check.

USUALLY FINE

Camera or sensor obstruction alerts

Often just a dirty lens or bad weather — clean the relevant camera first before assuming it's a hardware fault.

When in doubt, tap "Learn More"

Every alert links to a plain-language explanation and, where relevant, a way to request service directly — always check there before searching the internet for a diagnosis.

08

Long-Term Storage

Leaving the car for a few weeks or longer takes a little different care than daily use.

BEST PRACTICE

Leave it plugged in if possible

Staying connected to power — even without actively charging — lets the battery management system maintain a healthy state automatically while you're away.

Set a moderate charge limit before leaving

A mid-range charge level is better for long-term storage than leaving it at 100% or letting it sit very low for weeks at a time.

Check on it remotely if you can

The app lets you glance at charge level and car status remotely — worth a quick check partway through an extended trip.

Don't fully disconnect the 12V system unnecessarily

Unless specifically instructed by service for extended storage, leave standard systems as-is rather than improvising a disconnect.
