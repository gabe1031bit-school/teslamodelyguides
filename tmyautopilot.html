01

The Different Tiers

Three distinct products get lumped together in casual conversation — they aren't the same thing.

STANDARD

Basic Autopilot

Included on every car — traffic-aware cruise control and lane-centering assistance for highway driving. This is the baseline, not an upgrade.

PAID ADD-ON

Enhanced Autopilot

Adds features like automatic lane changes, smart parking assistance, and highway on/off-ramp navigation on top of the basic package.

PAID ADD-ON

Full Self-Driving (Supervised)

The most capable tier — adds city-street driving, traffic light and stop sign response, and automatic steering in more situations. Still requires full driver attention at all times, matching its "supervised" name.

Check current feature lists before buying

Exactly what's bundled into each tier has changed over time — always confirm the current feature list in the app or on the order page before purchasing.

02

What It Can & Can't Do

A realistic picture, not a marketing one.

GENUINELY GOOD AT

Reducing fatigue on long highway stretches

Lane-keeping and adaptive cruise handle the repetitive parts of long drives well, which is where most owners find the most day-to-day value.

GENUINELY GOOD AT

Consistent following distance

Traffic-aware cruise control tends to maintain following distance more consistently than most drivers do manually over a long drive.

STILL NEEDS YOU FOR

Unusual or ambiguous situations

Construction zones, unusual intersections, erratic other drivers, and unmarked roads are exactly where active driver attention matters most.

STILL NEEDS YOU FOR

Final judgment calls, always

Regardless of tier or marketing name, none of these systems make the car fully autonomous — the driver remains responsible for the vehicle at all times.

03

Staying Responsible

"Supervised" is in the name for a reason.

Hands and attention on the task

Keep hands ready on the wheel and eyes on the road — the car will prompt you if it detects insufficient attention, and repeated warnings can suspend access to the features.

Be ready to take over instantly

Treat every engaged drive as if you might need to intervene in the next second — because occasionally, you will.

Know your local laws

Rules around driver-assistance systems vary by state and country — it's worth knowing what's legally required in your area beyond what the car itself enforces.

Don't rely on it in poor conditions

Heavy rain, snow, or low visibility can all reduce these systems' effectiveness — the same conditions that call for more of your own attention, not less.

💡 Rule of thumb

Think of it as a very capable co-pilot, not a replacement driver — the moment you'd want a human co-pilot to speak up is the moment you should already be paying attention.

05

Keeping It Working Well

The system is only as good as its inputs.

Clean cameras regularly

Dirty or obstructed cameras are the most common reason these features become temporarily unavailable — a quick wipe often fixes it instantly.

Keep the windshield clear too

Some cameras look through the windshield — cracks, heavy grime, or aftermarket tint in the wrong spot can all interfere.

Install updates in a reasonable timeframe

Updates periodically refine these systems' behavior — staying reasonably current keeps you on the best-performing version.

Report odd behavior through the app

If the car does something unexpected, the in-app bug report captures useful data automatically — more helpful than trying to describe it from memory later.

06

Common Misconceptions

A few things worth clearing up before they cause a bad habit.

MYTH

"It's basically self-driving now"

Even the highest tier is officially supervised — the driver is still the one responsible for the vehicle's operation at all times.

MYTH

"Once it's on, I can relax"

The opposite is closer to true — active supervision is a requirement of using the feature, not an optional suggestion.

MYTH

"All Teslas drive the same way"

Behavior varies by which tier is active, current software version, and road conditions — two owners can have very different day-to-day experiences.

MYTH

"A disengagement means something's broken"

The system handing control back to you in an ambiguous situation is often it working as intended, not a malfunction.
