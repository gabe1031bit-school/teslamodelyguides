01

Genuine vs. Third-Party

Not every accessory needs to be the official one — but a few really do.

GET THIS RIGHT

Floor mats and liners

A properly fitted liner matters more than brand — an ill-fitting mat can bunch up near the pedals, which is a real safety issue, not just an aesthetic one.

Roof racks and crossbars

Fitment and weight rating matter here more than logo — a well-reviewed third-party rack rated for your model is generally fine, but always check the rated load limit.

Screen protectors and wraps

Mostly cosmetic and low-risk either way — pick based on reviews and finish preference rather than assuming genuine is automatically better.

LOWER STAKES

Phone mounts, organizers, sunshades

These are the safest categories to shop purely on price and reviews — little functional difference between genuine and well-made third-party versions.

02

Accessories Worth Buying

The ones that tend to get used every single day, not just the first week.

HIGH USE

All-weather floor liners

Protects the factory carpet from mud, snow melt, and spills — one of the few accessories nearly every owner ends up glad they bought.

HIGH USE

A proper trunk/cargo liner

Especially useful for anyone hauling groceries, pets, or gear regularly — protects the factory surface and is easy to wipe down.

Sunshade for the glass roof

Worth considering in hot climates — the expansive glass roof lets in a lot of heat and light without one.

A quality center console organizer

The stock console space is usable but loose — a well-fitted organizer keeps small items from sliding around on turns.

03

Ones You Can Probably Skip

Not bad products, necessarily — just ones that tend to sit unused after the novelty wears off.

OFTEN UNUSED

Elaborate ambient lighting kits

Fun for the first week, rarely mentioned by owners a year in — a cosmetic purchase more than a functional one.

Excessive badge or trim replacements

Purely cosmetic, and some installations risk damaging factory paint or clips if not done carefully — weigh the look against the risk.

Redundant phone chargers

Many trims already include built-in wireless charging — check what's already there before buying an add-on charger you don't need.

Cheap, ill-fitting seat covers

A poor-fitting cover can look worse than no cover at all, and cheap materials can trap heat uncomfortably — if you want covers, fit matters more than price.

04

Cargo & Storage

The space is generous — a little organization makes it genuinely useful.

Use the front trunk for delicate items

The frunk is a separate, secure, weatherproof space — good for groceries, luggage, or anything you don't want sliding around in the rear cargo area.

Fold rear seats for larger loads

The rear seats fold flat for significantly more cargo volume — worth remembering before ruling the car out for a bigger haul.

Cargo nets and dividers

Keep loose items from sliding during turns and stops — a small, inexpensive addition that prevents a surprising amount of rattling and shifting.

Know the underfloor storage

There's usable space beneath the rear cargo floor — handy for charging cables or emergency items you don't need daily access to.

05

Hidden Interior Features

Small details a lot of owners don't discover for months.

Ventilated or heated seats, by trim

Available seat comfort features vary by trim and options package — worth checking what your specific configuration includes rather than assuming.

Multiple USB-C ports, front and rear

Easy to miss on first inspection — check both rows and the console for full charging coverage on longer drives with passengers.

Adjustable ambient lighting (stock)

Built-in lighting customization exists in software before you ever need to buy an aftermarket kit — check the settings menu first.

Rear touchscreen controls, where equipped

On models with one, rear passengers can control climate and entertainment independently — worth pointing out to anyone unfamiliar riding in back.

06

Kids & Pets

A couple of settings make the cabin noticeably easier to live with for both.

Check car seat anchor points before buying

Confirm anchor locations and clearances with your specific seat model — fitment can vary more than expected between car seat brands.

Use Dog Mode in warm weather

A built-in climate-monitoring mode keeps the cabin at a set temperature and displays a message for passersby when a pet is briefly left in the car.

Child lock settings

Rear door and window child locks are adjustable in the touchscreen settings — worth setting up before the first drive with young kids.

Washable liners pay off fast

Between kids and pets, a wipeable cargo and seat liner setup saves far more cleaning effort than it costs upfront.
