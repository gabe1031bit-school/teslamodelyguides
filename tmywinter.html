01

Cold Weather Range

The drop is real, temporary, and normal battery chemistry — not a fault.

EXPECT THIS

Range drops in cold weather

Battery chemistry is simply less efficient when cold, and cabin heating draws additional energy — a noticeable range reduction in freezing temperatures is normal, not a sign of a problem.

It recovers once temperatures rise

The efficiency loss is temporary and tied to ambient and battery temperature — range returns to normal as things warm up, with no lasting effect on battery health.

Cabin heat costs more than seat heaters

Heating the whole cabin uses more energy than heating just the seats and steering wheel — using seat/wheel heat as your primary warmth source stretches range further.

Watch the Energy app in winter

It's especially useful this time of year for seeing exactly how much of your energy use is going to climate versus driving.

02

Preconditioning

The single best winter habit — warm the car while it's still plugged in.

BIGGEST LEVER

Precondition before you unplug

Warming the cabin and battery while still connected to power uses grid electricity instead of battery charge — you start your drive warm without paying range for it.

Schedule it to finish right on time

Set a departure time in the app or car so preconditioning finishes just as you're ready to leave, rather than running longer than needed.

A warm battery also means better regen

Regenerative braking is limited when the battery is cold — preconditioning helps restore full regen sooner into your drive.

Remote start from the app on cold mornings

No need to go outside first — start preconditioning from the app a few minutes before you head out.

03

Charging in the Cold

A cold battery charges slower — a couple of habits work around it easily.

Precondition before Supercharging

Routing to a Supercharger via in-car navigation warms the battery in advance, which noticeably speeds up charging on arrival versus showing up cold.

EXPECT THIS

Home charging is slower too

Overnight charging in very cold weather may take somewhat longer — plan slightly more charging time on the coldest nights if you need a full charge by morning.

Keep it plugged in overnight when very cold

Staying connected helps the car manage battery temperature automatically, even outside active charging windows.

Don't panic at a lower % after a cold soak

A charge level that looked fine the night before can read a bit lower on a very cold morning — this is normal and typically self-corrects once driving begins.

04

Tires & Traction

Nothing else on this page matters as much for safety as this section.

1Dedicated winter tiresThe single biggest traction improvement in snow and sustained cold below ~45°FBest option
2All-season tires, properly inflatedAdequate for light winter conditions if you're not seeing regular snow or iceAcceptable, climate-dependent
3Summer or worn tiresMeaningfully reduced grip in cold and wet conditions — avoid if winter driving is a regular part of your routineNot recommended

💡 Rule of thumb

Instant torque means an EV can accelerate confidently on snow — but it can't out-brake or out-corner physics on worn or wrong-season tires.

05

Charge Port & Door Care

A few seconds of prevention beats a frustrating frozen-shut morning.

Check the charge port isn't iced shut

After freezing rain or heavy snow, a quick look before you try to charge saves a struggle — a soft cloth and gentle pressure is safer than force.

Avoid slamming icy doors

Door seals can stick in freezing temperatures — open gently rather than forcing, and avoid excess force on the frunk in icy weather too.

Clear snow from door handles

Built-up snow or ice around handles is the most common source of "why won't my door open" on a snowy morning.

Wipe down seals occasionally in winter

A light wipe of door and trunk seals helps prevent them from freezing shut after wet, cold weather.

06

Snow & Ice Removal

Getting the car clear without scratching paint or damaging sensors.

USE CARE

Use a soft brush, not a scraper, on the body

Ice scrapers can scratch paint — a soft snow brush for the body and a proper plastic scraper only on glass avoids unnecessary damage.

Clear cameras and sensors first

Before driving, clear snow and ice from all cameras and sensors — Autopilot and safety features rely on a clear view to work properly.

Don't pour hot water on frozen glass

The temperature shock can crack cold glass — use the defrost system and a bit of patience instead.

Let defrost run before scraping

A few minutes of cabin defrost loosens ice on the windshield, making manual scraping faster and gentler on the glass.

07

Software Features That Help

A few built-in tools most owners forget exist until the first cold snap.

Defrost/defog mode

A dedicated setting that prioritizes clearing the windshield and windows quickly, separate from general cabin heating.

Scheduled departure with preconditioning

Combines a warm cabin with off-peak charging timing, so you leave warm without charging during expensive hours.

Range display adjusts as you warm up

Estimated range recalculates as the battery warms during your drive — the number you see cold-starting isn't your steady-state range.

Cabin overheat/cold protection settings

Optional settings that maintain a minimum cabin temperature while parked — useful if pets or sensitive cargo are ever left in the car briefly.

08

Winter Road Trips

Longer drives in cold weather just need slightly more margin, not a different plan.

Budget extra range margin

Plan for shorter real-world range than in mild weather, and let in-car navigation account for it when routing Supercharger stops.

Precondition before every fast-charge stop

This matters even more in winter, since a cold battery charges noticeably slower than a warmed one.

Pack a warm layer, not just chargers

Simple insurance — if you ever end up waiting roadside for any reason, being prepared for the cold matters as much as the car being prepared.

Check road and weather conditions ahead

Winter road trips benefit from checking conditions along the whole route, not just the destination, before you leave.
